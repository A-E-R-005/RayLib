1. The `Camera2D` Structure
	Raylib provides the `Camera2D` structure to define the view into your game world. It requires four main properties:

|**Property**|**Data Type**|**Purpose**|
|---|---|---|
|`offset`|`Vector2`|The translation applied to the screen (like a border or margin). This is usually half the screen size to center the camera.|
|`target`|`Vector2`|The point in the _game world_ the camera is focusing on (i.e., the player's position).|
|`rotation`|`float`|Rotation applied to the camera view (we use `0.0f`).|
|`zoom`|`float`|Zoom level of the camera (we use `1.0f`).|

2. The Camera Drawing Block
	To draw the world relative to the camera, all your drawing calls must be placed inside a special drawing bloc:

$BeginMode2D(camera) -> {World Drawing Logic} ->End Mode2D()$
	Anything drawn inside this block is interpreted as being drawn in World Coordinates, and the camera translates those coordinates to fit the screen.


3. Implementation Flow
	- Define Camera: Initialize a `Camera2D` object
	- Update Camera: In the Update phase, continuously update the camera's `target` to follow the player's position.
	- Draw World: Move all your existing drawing calls (`DrawTextureRec`, `DrawRectangle`, etc.) into the `BeginMode2D` / `EndMode2D` block.
